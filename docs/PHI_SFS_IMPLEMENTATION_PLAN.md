# Φ-SFS Wasserstein Implementation Plan

## 1. Objective

Replace the current Φ-SFS calculation with a first Wasserstein distance on the
derived-allele-frequency (DAF) axis, then calibrate that distance against neutral
SNP sets that have the same number of sites and are age matched to the focal TE
set.

The revised analysis reports both:

1. a raw, interpretable distance between the focal TE SFS and an age-matched SNP
   SFS; and
2. a category-specific Z-score and Monte Carlo P-value that account for the
   positive distance expected between two finite samples.

The genome-wide SNP controls carry the demographic history, linked selection,
ARG filters, polarization rules, callability, and data-quality filters shared
with the TE data. No explicit demographic model is required.

This is a planned replacement. Existing results from `normalize_tes.phi_sfs`
use the earlier definition and must not be combined with revised results.

## 2. Inputs and invariants

For each focal TE category, let $A$ contain $M$ sites. Generate one neutral
reference set $B_0$ and $R$ additional neutral sets
$B_1,\ldots,B_R$. Require that:

- every $B_i$ contains exactly $M$ SNPs;
- SNPs are age matched to the TEs using the existing posterior-age matching;
- all sets use the same individuals, polarization, callable-site rules, ARG
  filters, and data-quality filters;
- every $B_i$, including $B_0$, is generated by the same algorithm;
- $B_0$ is designated without inspecting its SFS;
- matching diagnostics and SNP reuse are retained; and
- focal and control spectra use the same projected DAF grid.

Use $R\ge1000$ for reported analyses. At $R=1000$, the minimum attainable
add-one Monte Carlo P-value is $1/1001$.

## 3. Constructing the unfolded SFS

### 3.1 Site-level projection

For a site with observed derived count $k$ among $n$ callable inbred
individuals, project to $m$ individuals with

$$
h_j(k,n)=\Pr(J=j\mid k,n,m)
=\frac{\binom{k}{j}\binom{n-k}{m-j}}{\binom{n}{m}},
\qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
$$

Evaluate this with a stable hypergeometric probability mass function. Do not
perform random downsampling. Exclude sites with $n<m$. Calculate endpoint
mass in bins 0 and $m$ for diagnostics, but compare only polymorphic bins
$1,\ldots,m-1$.

Do not renormalize individual site projections after dropping endpoints. Sum
site contributions within each complete set, then normalize that set's
polymorphic spectrum to one.

### 3.2 Set-level spectra

For any set $X$, calculate

$$
S_{Xj}=\sum_{i\in X}h_j(k_i,n_i),
\qquad
p_{Xj}=\frac{S_{Xj}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}S_{X\ell}}.
$$

Define its discrete CDF on $x_j=j/m$ as

$$
F_X(x_j)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}p_{X\ell}.
$$

## 4. New definition of Φ-SFS

Define Φ-SFS as the first Wasserstein distance between normalized spectra on
the DAF scale:

$$
\boxed{
\Phi_{\mathrm{SFS}}(X,Y)=W_1(X,Y)
=\int_0^1\left|F_X(x)-F_Y(x)\right|\,dx
}.
$$

On the equally spaced projected grid, compute

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{SFS}}(X,Y)
=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
\left|F_X(x_j)-F_Y(x_j)\right|.
$$

Using DAF rather than allele-count units fixes the scale across projection
sizes. The statistic lies in $[0,1]$, is zero for identical spectra, and grows
when more probability mass must move farther along the DAF axis.

Φ-SFS is unsigned. Retain $F_X-F_Y$, signed bin residuals $p_X-p_Y$, and
the difference in mean DAF to distinguish excess rare variants from excess
high-frequency derived variants.

## 5. Finite-site null calibration

### 5.1 Observed and null distances

Hold $B_0$ fixed and calculate

$$
D_{\mathrm{obs}}=\Phi_{\mathrm{SFS}}(A,B_0)
$$

and

$$
D_i^0=\Phi_{\mathrm{SFS}}(B_i,B_0),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,R.
$$

$D_i^0$ is a raw distance between two neutral SNP sets, not a Z-score. Since
all sets contain $M$ sites, its distribution estimates the positive distance
expected from finite sampling at the focal category's size and age distribution.

The common reference is intentional: under the neutral null, $A$ and each
$B_i$ are exchangeable relative to $B_0$, conditional on the matching design.
Repeat the calculation with several randomly selected reference sets as a
sensitivity check. Strong reference dependence warns that the control pool or
focal set is too small or heterogeneous.

### 5.2 Null-standardized effect size

Let $μ_0$ and $s_0$ be the mean and sample standard deviation of the
$D_i^0$. Define

$$
\boxed{Z_A=\frac{D_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mu_0}{s_0}}.
$$

Positive $Z_A$ means that the TE-to-SNP distance exceeds the typical
neutral-to-neutral distance for the same site count and matching design. Always
report $D_{\mathrm{obs}}$, $μ_0$, and $s_0$ with $Z_A$.

### 5.3 Monte Carlo P-value

Use the one-sided test for an unusually large distance:

$$
\boxed{
P_A=\frac{1+\sum_{i=1}^{R}
\mathbf{1}\!\left(D_i^0\ge D_{\mathrm{obs}}\right)}{R+1}
}.
$$

The add-one correction prevents a zero P-value. Report $R$ and the resulting
resolution. This P-value asks whether the focal SFS is farther from its matched
neutral background than expected between two finite neutral samples. It does
not identify the direction of the shift or estimate a selection coefficient.

## 6. Between-category comparisons

Category-specific Z-scores give a common effect-size scale, but visual separation
between two points is not a formal test. For categories $g=1,2$, calculate

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{obs}}=Z_{A_1}-Z_{A_2}.
$$

Standardize each category's null values and form null contrasts:

$$
Z_{gi}^0=\frac{D_{gi}^0-\mu_{0g}}{s_{0g}},
\qquad
\Delta_i^0=Z_{1i}^0-Z_{2i}^0.
$$

If the same control-generation seed or bootstrap draw has a shared meaning
across categories, preserve that pairing. Otherwise use prespecified random
pairing, repeated across several pairings, or all cross-category pairs. A
two-sided Monte Carlo contrast is

$$
P_\Delta=\frac{1+\sum_i
\mathbf{1}\!\left(|\Delta_i^0|\ge|\Delta_{\mathrm{obs}}|\right)}{R+1}.
$$

Prespecify contrasts and correct for multiple testing when examining many pairs.

## 7. Visualization and reporting

The definition figure should show the focal and neutral SFS above their CDFs,
with the area between CDFs representing Φ-SFS. Category summaries should:

- show each category-specific null distribution on the Z scale in gray;
- plot one equal-size point per focal TE category at $Z_A$;
- color points by $-\log_{10}P_A$, not by point size;
- use a 0–3 color scale for $R=1000$; and
- state the focal site count $M$.

For each category, publish:

- focal and reference SFS and CDF vectors;
- $M$, $R$, matching diagnostics, and reuse diagnostics;
- $D_{\mathrm{obs}}$, all $D_i^0$, $μ_0$, $s_0$, $Z_A$, and $P_A$;
- the reference-set index and random seeds;
- signed bin and CDF residuals plus mean-DAF difference; and
- reference-rotation sensitivity results.

## 8. Software changes

The revised implementation should:

1. require or generate $R+1$ equivalently sampled matched SNP sets;
2. enforce equal post-filter site counts across $A$ and every $B_i$, or apply
   a declared prespecified equalization rule;
3. retain the deterministic projection and set-level normalization;
4. replace the old scalar calculation with CDF area on the DAF scale;
5. designate $B_0$ before inspecting spectra;
6. calculate observed and null distances, Z, and the add-one P-value;
7. emit every raw quantity needed to reproduce the result; and
8. bump schema and algorithm versions so old and new results cannot be mixed.

A direct cumulative sum and weighted absolute difference is sufficient. A
generic optimal-transport solver is unnecessary on this ordered 1D grid.

## 9. Validation tests

### 9.1 Wasserstein calculation

- Identical spectra give Φ-SFS = 0.
- Point masses at DAF 0.05 and 0.95 give distance 0.90.
- Moving the same mass twice as far doubles its contribution.
- Direct CDF area agrees with a trusted 1D Wasserstein implementation.
- Results remain in DAF units when the projection grid changes.

### 9.2 Null calibration

- Exchangeable synthetic neutral data give uniform Monte Carlo P-values within
  simulation error.
- The null mean rises as $M$ falls, while calibrated P-values remain valid.
- Observed and null comparisons use exactly $M$ retained sites.
- The P-value is never zero and has minimum $1/(R+1)$.
- Known SFS shifts produce increasing Z and power as shift or site count grows.
- Reference rotation gives compatible conclusions with an adequate control pool.

### 9.3 Integration and provenance

- Focal and controls are bound to the same samples, filters, age target, and
  source store.
- A fixed seed reproduces reference selection, distances, Z, and P.
- Interrupted output cannot publish a result marked complete.
- New aggregation and plotting reject old-schema results.

## 10. Interpretation and limitations

Age matching handles the strong relationship between allele age and frequency
without specifying demography. Genome-wide SNP controls also absorb shared
demography, linked selection, ARG quality, and filtering effects to the extent
that controls experience them similarly to TEs.

The focal TE set remains one observed sample. Calibration removes the finite-
control sampling floor but cannot make a small or unusual focal set
representative. Dependence among TEs, imperfect matching, control reuse, local
genomic differences, polarization error, and inferred-age uncertainty can all
reduce the effective null information. The $R$ control sets are not independent
biological replicates.

The primary inferential result is the pair $(Z_A,P_A)$, accompanied by the raw
Φ-SFS distance and CDFs. Evidence for selection requires an unusually large
distance relative to the age- and size-matched neutral null plus a CDF shape
consistent with the proposed mode of selection.
